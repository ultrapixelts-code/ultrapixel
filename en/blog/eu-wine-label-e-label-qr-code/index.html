EU wine labels: ingredients, nutrition and the e-label QR code (Regulation (EU) 2021/2117)

Published November 5, 2026UltraPixel, Trieste2 min read
Short answer

For wines produced from 8 December 2023 and sold in the EU, Regulation (EU) 2021/2117 requires a list of ingredients and a nutrition declaration. The energy value and allergens must appear on the physical label; the full ingredient list and nutrition declaration can be given electronically through an e-label reached by a QR code, provided that page carries no marketing content and does not track users. For the printer, the critical point is a QR code that scans reliably on textured paper, near foil and on a curved bottle.

Key points
  • Energy value per 100 ml (which may be shown with the symbol E) and allergens stay on the bottle.
  • Ingredients and full nutrition can move to an e-label via QR code.
  • The e-label must not contain marketing or collect personal data.
  • Each wine and vintage may need its own QR code: plan versions and variable data.
  • Never print the QR code in foil or over texture; test scanning on the finished bottle.

Rules for the e-label page

  • It must show only the mandatory information, not sales or marketing content, and it must not be your general brand website.
  • It must not collect or track user data.
  • It must be available in a language easily understood by consumers in the countries where the wine is sold.
  • The QR code on the label should be clearly identified as leading to ingredient and nutrition information; check the Commission guidance and your national rules for the accepted wording.

Printing a QR code that scans on a premium label

RiskWhy it failsWhat to do
QR in foilMetallic reflections confuse phone camerasPrint the QR in dark ink on a light, flat area
QR on textured paperFelt or embossed texture breaks the modulesKeep the QR on a smooth area or check the stock with your printer
Too smallModules too small to resolve on a curved bottleAgree a minimum size with the printer and test on the bottle
No quiet zoneThe scanner cannot find the code's edgesLeave a clear margin around the code
Low contrastColoured or tinted backgrounds reduce readabilityDark code on a light background, never the reverse on dark stock without testing
CurvatureThe code bends on narrow bottlesPlace it on the widest part, oriented to minimise curvature

Versions, vintages and variable data

Ingredients and nutrition values can change between wines and vintages, so a winery with several references may need a different QR code on each label. With digital printing, different codes, vintages and versions can be printed in the same job. If the link is the same and only the destination page changes, a single code can serve several vintages, but the content must remain correct for each bottle on the market. See variable-data labels and short-run wine labels.

Frequently asked questions

Which wines must carry ingredient and nutrition information?

Wines produced from 8 December 2023 and sold in the EU. Wines produced before that date can be sold until stocks run out.

Can the QR code replace the whole wine label?

No. Energy value, allergens and the usual mandatory particulars must stay on the physical label; only the full ingredient list and nutrition declaration can be provided electronically.

Can the e-label page link to my shop or brand website?

The e-label must not contain marketing or sales content and must not track users. Keep it separate from your brand website.

Can a QR code be printed in gold foil?

It is not recommended: metallic reflections make scanning unreliable. Print it in dark ink on a flat, light area and test it on the finished bottle.
