PPWR and labels: what the EU Packaging Regulation means for self-adhesive labels (2026–2030)

Published November 9, 2026UltraPixel, Trieste3 min read
Short answer

The EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2025/40 (PPWR), applies from 12 August 2026. For labels the main milestones are: harmonised material-sorting labels on packaging from 12 August 2028 (or 24 months after the Commission's implementing act); compostable sticky labels on fruit and vegetables from 12 February 2028; and recyclability performance grades from 2030. Because recyclability is assessed on the pack as a whole, facestock, adhesive and decoration should be chosen together with the container.

Key points
  • PPWR is a regulation, so it applies directly in every EU member state.
  • Many PPWR obligations depend on implementing and delegated acts the Commission is still adopting: dates can shift.
  • Harmonised sorting labels will change label artwork on many packs: plan versions and stock.
  • Label material, adhesive and finishing all influence how a pack is recycled.
  • Avoid large stocks of pre-printed labels for packs whose artwork will have to change.

Why labels matter for recyclability

Under PPWR, recyclability is assessed on the packaging unit. A label is small, but its facestock, adhesive, inks and finishes can affect how a bottle, jar or tray is sorted and recycled: for example, whether the label separates in the washing process or how it behaves with the container material. The detailed design-for-recycling criteria are set in delegated acts, so the practical rule today is to choose the label together with the container and to ask suppliers for documentation on each material, not general claims.

Harmonised sorting labels: plan the artwork change

Article 12 of PPWR introduces harmonised labels that tell consumers how to sort the packaging, based on its material composition. When the implementing act is in place, many brands will have to update the artwork of every reference. For label buyers this means:

  • Keep label stocks low on references that will change, and plan reprints around the deadline.
  • Group artwork changes so that sorting labels, other regulatory updates and design changes go to print together.
  • Prefer production methods that make version changes inexpensive, such as digital printing for short and medium runs.

Questions to ask your label printer

  1. Which facestock and adhesive do you propose for my container, and what documentation exists for each?
  2. Which recycled-content or certified materials are available for this application?
  3. Which finishes are really needed, and which only add material?
  4. How do you handle many artwork versions when regulatory changes arrive?
  5. Can we test the proposed label on our containers and, where relevant, through our recycler's guidelines?

Frequently asked questions

When does PPWR apply?

Regulation (EU) 2025/40 entered into force on 11 February 2025 and generally applies from 12 August 2026. Several obligations have later dates or depend on implementing and delegated acts.

Do labels have to change because of PPWR?

Many packs will need harmonised sorting labels based on material composition, expected from 12 August 2028 or 24 months after the relevant implementing act. That usually means an artwork change on the label.

Do fruit and vegetable stickers have to be compostable?

Yes. PPWR requires sticky labels attached to fruit and vegetables to be compostable from 12 February 2028, subject to the conditions in the regulation.

Does the label affect whether my packaging is recyclable?

It can. Recyclability is assessed on the packaging as a whole, so label material, adhesive and decoration should be chosen with the container and the recycling stream in mind.
