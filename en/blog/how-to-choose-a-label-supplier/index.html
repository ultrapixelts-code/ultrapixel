How to choose a label supplier in Europe: a checklist for brands and buyers

Published November 9, 2026UltraPixel, Trieste3 min read
Short answer

Judge a label supplier on five things you can verify before the first order: the materials and adhesives they propose for your container and conditions, the finishes they can apply in register in one workflow, how they control colour between runs, how they handle versions and variable data, and whether they send samples you can test on your own packs. Price per thousand matters, but a label that lifts in an ice bucket or drifts in colour across a range costs more than it saves.

Key points
  • Ask for a material and adhesive proposal based on your container, surface and storage conditions, not a generic catalogue.
  • Check which finishes are done in-house and in register: foil, screen varnish, embossing, die-cutting.
  • Ask how colour is measured and kept consistent from one run to the next.
  • Test samples on your own bottles, jars or drums before ordering.
  • Compare total cost: reprints, waste, obsolete stock and line stoppages, not only the unit price.

What should a label supplier show you before the first order?

A serious supplier starts from your product, not from their price list. Before quoting, they should ask what the label goes on, how the product is filled, stored and used, and how the label is applied. The answer to those questions decides the facestock, the adhesive and the finishing, and therefore whether the label performs.

At minimum you should receive: a written material proposal (facestock + adhesive + liner), a description of each finish and how it is applied, a proof of the artwork with the die line, and physical samples you can test.

Which questions reveal real capability?

  1. Which material and adhesive would you use on my container, and why?
  2. Which finishes do you apply in-house, and can they be combined in register on the same detail?
  3. How do you measure colour, and what happens when a run drifts from the target?
  4. Can several versions, languages or vintages be printed in the same job?
  5. Can codes, numbers or QR codes change on every label?
  6. Which roll specifications do you need for my applicator (winding direction, core, outer diameter)?
  7. Will you send samples I can test on my own line before I order?

Vague answers to the first three questions are the clearest warning sign. They are the ones that decide whether the label works on the shelf.

Does it matter where in Europe the supplier is?

Within the EU, a label printed in another member state moves under the same single-market rules as a domestic one, so location matters less than many buyers assume. What does matter is clear communication in your language, realistic delivery planning and a supplier that understands the regulatory context of your market, such as EU packaging rules or wine labelling rules.

For brands with ranges sold in several countries, one supplier able to print all language versions with the same colour and finishing keeps the range consistent.

Red flags

  • A price before anyone has asked what the label goes on.
  • No samples, or samples on a different material from the one quoted.
  • Certifications or capacities claimed but not documented for your material or process.
  • No answer on how colour is measured.
  • Every finish outsourced, with no one owning register between them.

Frequently asked questions

How many label suppliers should I compare?

Two or three is usually enough if you give all of them the same specification: size, material, finishes, quantities, versions and roll data. Different specifications make prices impossible to compare.

Is the cheapest price per thousand the best deal?

Not necessarily. Reprints, waste, obsolete stock and line stoppages caused by the wrong material or roll specification often cost more than the difference in unit price.

Should I test labels before ordering?

Yes. Apply samples to your own containers in the real conditions of use (cold, humidity, oil, handling) before approving a material.

Can a European supplier print labels for several countries?

Yes. Language and market versions can be printed in the same job, keeping colour and finishing consistent across the range.
