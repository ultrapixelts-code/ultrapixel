How to request a label quote: the specification checklist that gets you an exact price

Published November 9, 2026UltraPixel, Trieste3 min read
Short answer

An exact label quote needs eleven pieces of information: size and shape, quantity per version and number of versions, material (or the container and conditions so the printer can propose one), finishes, colours, how the label is applied with roll data (winding direction, core, outer diameter, labels per roll), variable data, artwork format and delivery. Missing items are the main reason quotes from different printers cannot be compared.

Key points
  • Quote per version: 10,000 labels in one design and 10,000 split across ten designs are different jobs.
  • Describe the container and conditions; let the printer propose the material and adhesive.
  • For machine application, winding direction, core and outer diameter are not optional.
  • Send vector artwork with the die line on its own layer.
  • Send the same specification to every supplier you compare.

Why quantity per version matters

Two orders of 10,000 labels can have very different prices. One design printed 10,000 times has one set-up. Ten designs of 1,000 each, such as ten wines or ten fragrances, have ten. Digital printing reduces the cost of switching between versions, which is why short runs, vintages, languages and limited editions are usually printed digitally. Always state the number of versions and the quantity of each.

Roll data for automatic labelling machines

If your labels are applied by a machine, the roll must match it. Ask your production team or the applicator's manual for:

  • Winding direction: which edge of the label comes off the roll first, and whether labels face out or in. Printers usually refer to a standard set of numbered winding positions; a photo of a current roll on the machine is the safest reference.
  • Core diameter: the inner diameter of the cardboard core.
  • Maximum outer diameter: the largest roll the unwinder accepts.
  • Labels per roll or maximum roll length, and the gap between labels if the machine requires a specific one.

Artwork that saves a round of corrections

  • Vector PDF with fonts outlined or embedded.
  • The die line drawn on its own layer or as a named spot colour, not merged into the artwork.
  • Each finish (foil, varnish, relief, white) on its own layer or spot colour.
  • Bleed beyond the die line, and important text kept away from the edge.
  • Images at a resolution suited to the printed size.

Frequently asked questions

Why are label quotes from different printers so different?

Usually because they are quoting different specifications: another material, fewer finishes, a different roll format or a different number of versions. Send every supplier the same checklist.

Do I need to choose the label material myself?

No. Describe the container, surface and conditions of use and ask the printer to propose material and adhesive, then test samples.

What is winding direction?

It describes how the labels sit on the roll: which edge comes off first and whether the printed side faces out or in. Automatic applicators need the right one to work.

Which file format should I send?

A vector PDF, with the die line and each finish on separate layers or spot colours.
