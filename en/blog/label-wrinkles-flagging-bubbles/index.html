Labels wrinkling, flagging or bubbling: causes and fixes

Published November 9, 2026UltraPixel, Trieste2 min read
Short answer

Wrinkles usually mean the label shape does not match the container (a rectangle on a tapered surface) or the material is too stiff for the curve. Flagging, edges lifting, happens when a stiff facestock fights a small diameter or the adhesive is too weak for the surface. Bubbles and blisters come from trapped air, surface contamination, or gas released by some plastic containers under non-breathable film. The fix is usually a combination of shape, material, adhesive and application settings.

Key points
  • Tapered or conical containers need a label shape designed for the cone, not a rectangle.
  • On small diameters, use a more conformable facestock and a higher-tack adhesive; paper grain direction matters.
  • Blisters that appear hours after labelling on plastic containers often point to the container, not the label.
  • Dust, condensation and mould-release agents on containers reduce adhesion.
  • Test the final combination on real containers and wait before judging: some defects appear later.

Tapered bottles and conical jars

A rectangle can only sit flat on a cylinder. On a cone the top and bottom edges have different circumferences, so a rectangular label wrinkles or skews. The label must be designed as an arc, following the cone. Ask the label supplier to draw the die line on the real container.

Flagging on small diameters

Every facestock has a memory that wants to return flat. On small diameters, like vials, pens, small cosmetic bottles and pipettes, that force can beat the adhesive and the edge lifts, often hours later. Solutions: a thinner or more conformable material, an adhesive with higher tack, and for paper labels the grain running around the container. Overlapping wrap-around labels also benefit from an adhesive that bonds well to the label face itself.

Bubbles and blisters on plastic containers

Bubbles seen immediately are usually trapped air: wipe-down pressure or speed. Blisters that grow hours later on some plastic containers can be caused by gas released by the plastic, trapped under a film that does not let it escape. In that case the container, its treatment and the choice of facestock and adhesive have to be reviewed together with the label supplier and the container supplier.

Surface condition matters

  • Condensation on cold-filled containers: see ice-bucket and chilled labels.
  • Dust or mould-release agents on new containers.
  • Oils or product residues on food and cosmetic containers.
  • Containers labelled too soon after moulding or washing.

Frequently asked questions

Why do my labels wrinkle on the bottle?

Usually because the label shape does not match the container (for example a rectangle on a tapered surface) or the material is too stiff for the curve.

What is label flagging?

Label edges lifting away from the container, typical on small diameters when the facestock is too stiff or the adhesive too weak.

Why do bubbles appear on labels hours later?

On some plastic containers gas released by the container can be trapped under a non-breathable label. Container treatment and material choice need to be reviewed together.

Does paper grain direction matter?

Yes. On cylindrical containers, paper labels with the grain running around the circumference conform better and flag less.
